# Threshold & VO2max Development

Sources: Kristian Blummenfelt / Norwegian method (double-threshold), Eliud Kipchoge (80/20), Stephen Seiler / Norwegian 4×4, Peter Attia & Michael Joyner (VO2max).

## Intensity distribution

- **Pyramidal / polarized:** ~**80% low intensity, 10% moderate, 10% high.** Most volume is genuinely easy; quality work is purposeful and limited.

## Blummenfelt double-threshold protocol

The signature Norwegian-method block: two threshold sessions in one day, controlled by lactate, never all-out.

- **Cadence:** double-threshold days typically **Tuesday & Thursday**; **2×/week** during build.
- **Morning:** **5×6 min at 2.5–3.0 mmol/L lactate**, 1-min recovery.
- **Evening:** build to **4×10 min** or **10×1000 m at ~3.5 mmol/L lactate**, 1-min recovery.
- **Intensity feel:** controlled threshold (~60-min race pace), **not** maximal. The point is high-quality volume at threshold while preserving the aerobic base.

## Kipchoge 80/20 philosophy

- **~80% easy** running — truly conversational, genuinely easy.
- Quality sessions are specific, purposeful, and well-recovered.
- **Long runs every 2 weeks** (not weekly), starting **~4 min/mile slower than marathon pace** and progressing.
- Consistency over heroics — "only the disciplined are truly free."

## Norwegian 4×4 (VO2max)

- **4 min at 85–95% max HR / 4 min active recovery.**
- **4–6 intervals** per session.
- **1–2×/week** (this is the "20%" high-intensity volume).
- Evidence: meta-analytic improvements averaging **~0.5 L/min** VO2max with proper training.
- VO2max is one of the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality and underpins sustainable race pace.

## Attia VO2max prescription

- **3-min intervals at maximum *sustainable* effort** (hard but not a sprint).
- **3-min recovery** between intervals.
- **4–8 rounds** by fitness.
- **~1×/week** during build/peak.

## Application notes

- Lactate-guided sessions assume a meter; without one, RPE/pace anchors are approximate — flag this.
- High-intensity work belongs in **build/peak**, layered on a developed aerobic base — not piled on early or stacked on consecutive days.
