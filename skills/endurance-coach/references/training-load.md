# Training Load Management

Source: TrainingPeaks performance-management metrics + periodization/tapering science.

## Core metrics

- **TSS (Training Stress Score):** stress of a single workout = intensity × duration (relative to threshold). One hour at threshold ≈ 100 TSS.
- **CTL (Chronic Training Load):** **42-day** weighted average of daily TSS = **fitness**.
- **ATL (Acute Training Load):** **7-day** weighted average of daily TSS = **fatigue**.
- **TSB (Training Stress Balance):** **CTL − ATL** = **form / freshness** (yesterday's CTL−ATL).

## Optimal management targets

- **CTL ramp rate:** **5–8 points/week** for sustainable fitness building.
- **Build-phase TSB:** **−10 to −30** (productive overload — you should be carrying fatigue).
- **Race-day TSB:** **+15 to +25** after a proper taper.
- **Warning signs:** ramp **>10 points/week** → injury risk; TSB **< −30** → overreaching territory.

## Periodization framework

- **Base (8–12 weeks):** high volume, low intensity; build the CTL foundation. (See `zone2-aerobic.md`.)
- **Build (8–12 weeks):** add race-specific intensity (threshold + VO2max) while maintaining the CTL ramp. (See `threshold-vo2max.md`.)
- **Peak (2–3 weeks pre-race):** CTL at its maximum; highest training stress, race-specific work.
- **Taper (2–3 weeks):** **reduce volume 40–60%**, **maintain intensity**; let TSB rise to the race-day target. Sharpness is kept by intensity; freshness comes from cutting volume.

## Application notes

- These metrics are most meaningful with consistent data (power meter, HR, or pace-based TSS). Without devices, treat them qualitatively — relative fatigue vs. fitness.
- The numbers are guardrails, not laws — individual ramp tolerance varies; younger/experienced athletes may sustain higher ramps, masters athletes often less.
- Rising ATL with stagnant/falling performance + poor sleep/mood/HRV is an overtraining flag — back off before chasing the ramp.
