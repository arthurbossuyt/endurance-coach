# Zone 2 & Aerobic Development

Sources: Iñigo San Millán (Zone 2 / mitochondrial function), Peter Attia (80/20 polarized model).

## San Millán's Zone 2 protocol

- **Definition (gold standard):** blood lactate **1.7–1.9 mmol/L**. Accurate determination requires a lactate meter — this is the only precise way to set the zone.
- **Proxy without testing:** ~**60–70% of max HR**; conversational pace that "feels strained but sustainable."
- **Talk test:** can speak in complete sentences, but not hold a comfortable extended conversation.
- **Prescription:** **45–90 min per session, 3–4×/week**, minimum **3–4 hours/week** total.
- **Adaptation timeline:** **8–12 weeks** of consistent training for full mitochondrial adaptations.

### Adaptations driven by Zone 2

- Mitochondrial biogenesis (more, denser mitochondria).
- Enhanced fat oxidation — **0.6–1.0 g/min** in trained athletes.
- Improved lactate clearance (MCT transporter upregulation).
- Metabolic flexibility (efficient switching between fat and carbohydrate).

## Peter Attia's 80/20 integration

- **~80%** of weekly cardio in Zone 2.
- **~20%** in VO2max / high-intensity work (see `threshold-vo2max.md`).
- Framed as foundational for both performance **and** metabolic health / longevity.

## Critical caveats (always surface these)

- **HR estimates can be ±20 bpm off** without testing. A formula-derived zone is a starting guess, not a precise zone.
- **RPE alone is unreliable** — most self-coached athletes drift too hard and end up in "garbage" Zone 3 (too hard to build base, too easy to drive top-end adaptation). The classic error is easy days not being easy enough.
- Adaptations are slow and cumulative — consistency over 8–12 weeks matters more than any single session.
