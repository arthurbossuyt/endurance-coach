# Strength & Mobility for Endurance

Sources: Mike Israetel / Renaissance Periodization (volume landmarks), Ben Patrick / ATG ("Knees Over Toes").

## Israetel — Renaissance Periodization for endurance athletes

- **Volume landmarks:** **MEV** (Minimum Effective Volume) → **MAV** (Maximum Adaptive Volume) → **MRV** (Maximum Recoverable Volume).
- **Prescription for endurance athletes:** **2–3 strength sessions/week in the MEV–MAV range** — deliberately *not* full bodybuilding volume, because swim/bike/run already consume recovery. Total training stress is the budget.
- **Focus:** compound movements that support running economy — single-leg work, posterior chain, core stability.
- **Timing:** place strength **after easy runs or on rest days, never before a quality session.** Avoid compromising the key endurance workouts (concurrent-training interference).

## Ben Patrick — ATG / Knees Over Toes foundation

Targets the lower-leg and knee resilience endurance athletes commonly neglect.

- **Frequency:** **3×/week, 10–20 min.**
- **Core exercises:**
  - **Tibialis raises** — 25 reps (anterior tibialis).
  - **FHL calf raises** — 25 reps/leg (soleus focus).
  - **KOT calf raises** — 25 reps/leg (full-ROM calf).
  - **Patrick step-ups** — 25/leg (VMO / knee stability).
  - **ATG split squats** — 5×5/leg (knee resilience).
  - **Couch stretch & elephant walk** — hip-flexor / hamstring mobility.
- **Philosophy:** "For distance running, care about the soleus and anterior tibialis like lifters care about bench press." Builds joint resilience and addresses common lower-leg weakness.

## Integration principle

Strength and mobility are **supportive**, not the main dish. Protect the endurance sessions: keep lifting volume modest, schedule it so it doesn't sabotage quality run/bike/swim work, and progress gradually.
